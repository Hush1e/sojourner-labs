Part 1 of 3: Cybersecurity Awareness

The Urgent Email

Phishing emails rarely look like obvious spam anymore. They copy company branding, create pressure to act fast, and send you to a fake login page. The sender's address is one of the most reliable clues.

It is 8:46 on a Monday morning. This message lands in your inbox:

From: IT Support Desk <helpdesk@brightline-supply-support.com> To: all-staff@brightlinesupply.com Subject: URGENT: Password Reset Required Today Attention Employee, Due to a security update, all staff must confirm their login through the link below or their account will be suspended at 5:00 PM today. Confirm Your Account: http://brightline-login-verify.net/reset IT Support Desk

Your task: Type the email domain the message came from. This is the part of the sender's address after the @ sign.

Need a hint?Compare the sender's domain to the official company domain at the top of this page. Close is not the same.
What you caught
  • The sender domain is a look-alike, not brightlinesupply.com.
  • The greeting is generic: "Attention Employee."
  • It creates a deadline to rush you: "suspended at 5:00 PM today."
  • The link goes to a different website entirely and does not use https.

What to do: Do not click. Report the message to your manager or IT using a phone number or address you already know, not one in the email.

Part 2 of 3: AI Awareness

The Safe Prompt

When you type into a public AI tool, that information leaves your company. Depending on the tool and its settings, it may be stored, reviewed by the provider, or used to improve the product. The safe habit is the Placeholder Method: replace real names, numbers, and private details with generic labels like [Customer Name] before you paste anything.

You need help writing a polite reminder to a customer whose invoice is overdue. Three coworkers drafted prompts for a free online AI assistant. Which one is safe to use?

Prompt A: "Write a firm email to Dana Whitfield at 412 Elm Street, Athens, Ohio. Her account #88213 is 60 days past due for $4,312.50."Reference code: P-4402
Prompt B: "Write a polite reminder email to [Customer Name] about an invoice for [Amount] that is [Number] days past due. Keep it friendly and offer a payment plan."Reference code: P-7319
Prompt C: "Here is our full customer spreadsheet. Find everyone who is past due and write each of them a reminder email."Reference code: P-1185

Your task: Enter the reference code of the safe prompt.

Need a hint?Look for the prompt that could be read by a stranger without revealing anything about a real person.
Why Prompt B is safe

Prompt B gets the same useful result with no real data. You fill in the name and amount yourself after the AI writes the draft. Prompt A exposes a customer's name, address, account number, and balance. Prompt C hands over an entire customer database. Also remember that AI tools can make mistakes, so always read and check what they write before you send it.

Part 3 of 3: Compliance

The Auditor's Request

Personal information includes names combined with things like Social Security numbers, bank account numbers, driver's license numbers, and health information. State laws require businesses to protect this data and to notify people when it is exposed. How you send it matters as much as how you store it.

An outside auditor asks your manager for a file containing customer names, mailing addresses, and bank routing and account numbers. Which way of sending it protects the data?

Method 1: Attach the spreadsheet to a regular email and send it to the auditor's address.Method code: M-1
Method 2: Save the file in the company's approved secure file sharing system, limit access to the auditor only, require sign in with multi-factor authentication, and set the link to expire after the audit.Method code: M-2
Method 3: Copy the file to a USB drive and mail it in an envelope.Method code: M-3

Your task: Enter the method code that protects the data.

Need a hint?Which option controls who can open the file, and for how long?
Why Method 2 is correct

Method 2 limits access to one person, verifies who they are, and removes access when the job is done. Regular email can be forwarded, misdirected, or intercepted, and an unencrypted USB drive can be lost in the mail. If you are ever unsure how to send sensitive data, ask before you send.
